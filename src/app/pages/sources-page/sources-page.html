How we know how the sea feels

Bahari Leo listens to the weather and the water along Kenya’s coast, then says it in ordinary words. It will never show you where the fish are swimming. Nobody has that map.

What you are looking at

The story on each pin
Wave size, wind, and how warm the water is — turned into “kind”, “restless”, or “better from the sand”. The numbers hide behind a tap if you want them.
Green water and warm patches
Pictures from NASA, usually a day or two old. Green means tiny plants in the water. Warm colour means bath-warm sea. Fish like both. That is a hint, not a promise.
Protected reefs
Parks and reserves drawn so you don’t fish there by accident. Come to look, not to take.

Who talks to the sea for us

Open-Meteo
Hour-by-hour waves, wind, and water temperature. Free, no account.
NASA
Daily photos of the ocean’s colour and warmth.
Today’s edition
A Cloud Function reads the same forecast, then Gemini writes a short comic caption strip. If that step is quiet, you still get the ordinary words from the water.
For the curious

The old fishing-score is still there under the hood (waves, wind, 26–29.5°C water, current). Waves above chest-high, or wind that could roll a chair, always mean stay on land. Later we may add industrial boat tracks and catch reports — those need partner keys, so they are not live yet.
